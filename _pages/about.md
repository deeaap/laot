**Lab description**

Established in January 2026, the Laboratory of Animal Ontogeny across Time-Space (LAOT, the Filipino word for “open water”) dives deep into animal development and patterning across different spatial (e.g. molecule-, cell-, tissue-, organism-) and temporal (e.g. second-, minute-, hour-, day-) scales. LAOT builds on prior experience integrating developmental biology (devo) with evolution (evo), ecology (eco), theoretical and mathematical biology, physics, data science (e.g. -omics, timeseries, and high-content bioimage analyses), and instrumentation (e.g. advanced microscopy and microfluidics). We take this interdisciplinary approach to navigate the metaphorical ebbs and flows (i.e. spatiotemporal dynamics) that guide animal development along its typical voyage (i.e. “normal” development) and map where it is headed (e.g. novel trait, disease) when set on a different course in response to evolutionary and/or ecological factors.

As part of the Devo-Evo-Eco of Aquatic Animals of the Philippines ([deeaap](https://deeaap.github.io)) Unit, inspired by the rich aquatic biodiversity in the Philippines, we focus our studies on aquatic animals that are endemic and/or native to the archipelago. We are eager to practise developmental biology research that is adapted and specified to the Philippine context, promote knowledge equity, and resist intellectual imperialism.

**Ongoing research**

- Embryonic development, agametic propagation, and regeneration of endemic and/or native aquatic invertebrates (e.g. marine segmented worms)
- Embryonic and post-embryonic (e.g. metamorphosis) development of endemic and/or native fishes (e.g. *Caranx ignobilis* maliputo), with reference to the development of their patterns, bones, and muscles, in collaboration with [Dr. Stefano Davide Vianello](https://deeaap.github.io/gutphil)
